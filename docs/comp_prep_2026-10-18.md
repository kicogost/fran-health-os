## The core logic

Four things drive this plan:

1. **Mat time is the priority.** Everything else (strength, bike, mobility) is built around protecting the quality of your four mat sessions, not competing with them.
2. **Thursday and Sunday are the recovery levers.** You train five days with a hard Friday and a long Saturday. Those two rest days are what make the block sustainable. Protect them.
3. **Volume comes down as intensity goes up.** You peak by getting fresher, not by grinding harder in October.
4. **You arrive at weight, you don't cut to it.** More on this below.

## The 8-week arc

### Block 1: Base (Weeks 1 to 2, Aug 24 to Sep 6)

**Goal:** build work capacity and get your body used to five training days.

- Mat: broad. Positional sparring over free rolling. Start narrowing what your actual competition game is.
- Strength: full sessions, slow tempo, quality over reps.
- Bike: your normal range, 60 to 70km with the full elevation. This is your biggest aerobic block, use it.
- Weight: start tracking honestly. Aim for a slow downward drift, nothing aggressive.

### Block 2: Build (Weeks 3 to 5, Sep 7 to Sep 27)

**Goal:** highest total intensity of the camp. This is where the fitness gets built.

- Mat: hard rounds Tuesday and Friday. Start doing 6-minute rounds with short rests to mirror match length.
- Add a fifth round when you'd normally stop. Being the person who is still sharp in round five is how matches get won.
- Strength: same two sessions, but add explosive work (broad jumps, clap push-ups, sprawl-to-sprint).
- Bike: 50 to 70km. Keep it zone 2. If Friday open mat is wrecking you, cap Saturday at 50km.
- Weight: by end of Week 5 you should be within 2kg of your competition weight.

### Block 3: Sharpen (Weeks 6 to 7, Sep 28 to Oct 11)

**Goal:** competition-specific. Volume drops, intensity stays high.

- Mat: run tournament simulations. Two or three 6-minute matches with 10 to 15 minutes between them, exactly like a bracket. Do this at least twice in this block, ideally on a Friday.
- Drill your A-game only. No new techniques from here.
- Strength: cut to one session per week, power and grip only, 20 minutes.
- Bike: 40 to 50km, reduce elevation.
- Weight: you should be at or under 77kg by the end of Week 7. Comfortably.

### Block 4: Taper (Week 8, Oct 12 to Oct 18)

**Goal:** arrive fresh, sharp, and light. You cannot get fitter this week, you can only get tired.

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon 12 | BJJ, technical + light positional. 60% effort. |
| Tue 13 | BJJ, last day of hard rounds. Three or four good rounds, then stop. |
| Wed 14 | BJJ, drilling only. Movement, warm-up flows, your three highest-percentage sequences. |
| Thu 15 | OFF |
| Fri 16 | 30 min light drilling or full rest. No sparring. |
| Sat 17 | **No long ride.** 40 min very easy spin on flat ground, or take the day off entirely. |
| Sun 18 | **Compete.** |

Skipping the Saturday ride is the single most important change in this week. Tell your father in advance so you're not negotiating it the night before.

## Game plan: white belt no-gi ruleset

Straight ankle locks only, no reaping, no heel hooks or kneebars. This narrows the submission map a lot, and that's genuinely useful information because it tells you exactly where to spend your remaining drilling time.

### Treat legs as a sweeping system, not a finishing system

A straight ankle lock without reaping is low percentage against a resisting opponent who knows to turn away. But ashi garami and single leg X are excellent for **sweeps**, which are 2 points, and for breaking someone's base when they're passing.

So: enter leg entanglements to off-balance and sweep. If the ankle lock is there, take it. Don't hunt for it.

### Reaping is a disqualification, not a penalty

This deserves its own drilling attention. Under IBJJF rules reaping is an instant DQ, not a warning, and it's an easy accident when you're tired and scrambling. Two practical rules:

- Your outside foot stays on their **hip**, never across the centerline of their body
- **No saddle, no inside sankaku, ever.** Not in training either, because you don't want the motor pattern

Ask a training partner or coach to call reaping on you during live rounds through Blocks 2 and 3 until avoiding it is automatic.

The flip side: nobody can heel hook you either, so entering and defending leg entanglements is far lower risk than it would be at brown belt. You can afford to play there.

### Where your submissions actually live

The front headlock game is the single highest-value investment for white belt no-gi. At this level almost everyone shoots badly and leaves their head down, and the front headlock gives you three legal finishes plus a route to the back:

- **Guillotine** (high, arm-in, and low elbow variants)
- **D'Arce and anaconda**
- **Back take** off the failed guillotine when they posture out

Plus the standard core: rear naked choke, triangle, armbar, kimura, arm triangle from mount. That's more than enough. Pick three and get very good at them.

### Points will decide more of your matches

With the submission game narrowed, more matches go to the scoreboard. Wrestling becomes disproportionately valuable: takedown 2, sweep 2, guard pass 3, mount 4, back 4.

Spend real time in Blocks 1 and 2 on takedowns, especially the ability to finish a single leg and to re-shoot when the first attempt fails. Winning the first exchange and passing once is a 5 point lead, and most white belt matches don't come back from that.

### Also confirm before you compete

White belt rulesets usually restrict more than just legs. Wristlocks, neck cranks, slicers, scissor takedowns and jumping to closed guard are commonly illegal at this level too. Download the actual rules PDF for your specific event and read the banned techniques list yourself rather than relying on what your training partners tell you.

## Strength sessions (calisthenics)

Keep these to 25 minutes. They are a supplement, not a second workout. If a session would compromise Tuesday's rolls, cut it.

### Strength A (Monday)

Superset the first two. It saves time and the push and pull don't interfere with each other.

- Weighted or slow-tempo pull-ups: 4 x 5
- Pseudo-planche push-ups (hands at ribs, lean forward over them): 3 x 8
- Bulgarian split squats or pistol progressions: 3 x 6 per leg
- Towel pull-ups (hang from a towel over the bar): 3 x 6
- Nordic curl negatives: 3 x 4 (knee and hamstring insurance)
- Hollow hold: 3 x 30s

### Strength B (Wednesday)

- Dips, weighted if bodyweight is easy: 4 x 6
- Archer or deficit push-ups, 3s down and 1s pause at the bottom: 3 x 8 per side
- Single-leg Romanian deadlift (bodyweight or backpack loaded): 3 x 8 per leg
- Dead hangs, towel or thick bar: 3 x max time
- Copenhagen plank: 3 x 20s per side (adductors, directly transfers to guard retention)

Neck is not listed here because you already train it three times a week with partners. Do not stack more on top.

### Two things worth over-investing in

**Grip endurance.** No-gi grips fail differently than gi grips. Wrist control, collar ties, and overhooks all live or die on forearm endurance. Dead hangs and towel work are boring and they will win you matches.

**Neck.** Already covered, three times a week with partners. Two cautions: partner-applied resistance is inconsistent because they can't feel your fatigue, so you set the load and you call the stop. And taper it like everything else, no hard bridging after the Tuesday of competition week. If you get headaches, or any tingling or numbness in your arms or hands, stop and get it looked at rather than training through it.

## Weight: arrive, don't cut

**Where you actually are:** 78.45kg on 21 August. The limit is 77.0kg, and no-gi weigh-ins are done wearing shorts and a rashguard, which is roughly 0.3 to 0.4kg. So your real target is about **76.5kg naked**, which makes the gap around **1.9kg across 8 weeks**.

That is roughly 0.25kg per week. It is close to nothing, and the increase in training volume alone (going from three sessions a week to five) will likely cover most of it without you changing how you eat.

**Do not chase 74kg for this competition.** That's a 4.5kg loss during your hardest training block, and it would cost you strength, recovery and probably the camp. 74kg lean is a good off-season body composition goal. Revisit it in November.

**The approach:**

- Let the training volume do most of the work. Five sessions a week plus a long ride is already a significant energy expenditure.
- A modest, sustainable deficit is enough. Losing more than roughly 0.5kg per week during a hard camp will cost you strength and recovery, which is the opposite of what you want.
- Keep protein high and carbohydrates adequate. Under-eating carbs during Block 2 is the fastest way to have a flat, disappointing camp.
- **Be at competition weight by the end of Week 7,** a full week early. Then hold it.
- **Do not water cut.** Dehydration hurts grip strength, reaction time, and cognition, which is a bad trade in a sport decided by grip and decision-making. It also raises injury risk. If you're arriving from 78kg you don't need one, and if you'd need one, you're in the wrong division.

**Check the weigh-in format before you build any of this in.** Same-day weigh-ins right before your division are common in no-gi, and you weigh in wearing shorts and a rashguard, not stripped. Some events weigh in the day before. It changes your last 48 hours meaningfully.

## Logistics to sort now, not in October

- **Register early.** Divisions fill and late fees are real.
- **Confirm the ruleset.** Leg lock legality varies enormously between IBJJF no-gi, ADCC rules, and submission-only events, and it varies by belt. Know exactly what's legal for you and drill your defenses accordingly.
- **Skin checks.** Ringworm and staph get people pulled at the check-in table. Shower immediately after every session, wash your rashguards after every use.
- **Sleep is the highest-leverage thing on this list.** Eight hours through Blocks 2 and 3 will do more than any supplement.
- **Competition day kit:** two rashguards and two pairs of shorts, food you've already tested, water, tape, and mouthguard.

## What to track

Keep this stupidly simple. One note per week:

- Bodyweight, same conditions, two or three mornings per week
- Resting heart rate on waking (a rising trend across a week means you need a lighter one)
- Subjective energy, 1 to 10, after each mat session
- Anything that hurts, and for how long

If two of those four go red at the same time, take an extra rest day. That is not falling behind, it's the plan working.
